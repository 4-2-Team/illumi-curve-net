What changed since report 1

Fix 1 — masked exposure loss. L_exp used to pull every 16×16 patch's brightness toward a fixed target (0.6), fighting the mostly-black sky in these frames. L_exp_masked (new, in utils/losses.py) only applies that pull to patches that are at least 15% as bright as the frame's own brightest patch — i.e. the illuminated disc — and leaves sky patches alone.

Fix 3 — synthetic low-light training data. The source images are well-exposed NASA renders, not real low-light photos. synthetic_low_light() (new, in utils/dataloader.py) applies a random gamma raise + exposure cut + sensor-like noise to every training image, so the network is trained on the kind of dim, noisy frame it's actually meant to enhance, instead of one that doesn't need enhancing.

Both changes are individually well-motivated. Trained together with the same loss weights as report 1 (unchanged, to isolate their effect), the result was a clear regression — see below. That regression is diagnostic, not a dead end: it's the evidence that motivated the loss-weight rebalance in report 3.

Findings & caveats

  • ✗Both fixes backfired when combined at the original loss weights. Every IQA metric gets worse than baseline — BRISQUE +2.4%, PIQE +44.4% — and worse than report 1's naive fine-tune too (PIQE +25.3% vs. v1). This holds on plain test images and on synthetically degraded ones, so it isn't just a train/test mismatch from only training on degraded data.
  • •The mechanism is visible in the loss breakdown: masking the exposure loss made L_exp much easier to satisfy (−44% vs. baseline), which freed the optimizer to trade away spatial and texture fidelity for a lower total loss — L_texture rose 181% and L_spa rose 165%. The training objective went down; perceptual quality did not follow it down.
  • •Early stopping triggered this time (epoch 21, vs. report 1's full 100 with no plateau) — synthetic noise augmentation is acting as an effective regularizer, even though the resulting model is worse by every quality metric measured here.
  • •This motivates report 3 directly: L_texture and L_spa need more weight (they're currently the least-weighted terms, 3.0 and 1.5) to stop the optimizer from sacrificing them, and L_TV — already trivially near-zero under the original weights — can afford to lose most of its outsized weight (200.0) without cost. L_exp's weight (10.0) was calibrated for the harder, unmasked objective and likely no longer needs to be that high now that masking made the objective itself easier.
